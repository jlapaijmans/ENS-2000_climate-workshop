Introduction

In this practical, you will extract climate information from a dataset of coordinates. You will map your sample locations, look at the climate values associated with them, compare two groups, and carry out a statistical test.

NOTE: This practical is designed to work on the web version of Rstudio on rstudio.bangor.ac.uk. If you feel strongly about running it locally, you will need to install the pastclim package and its dependencies.

Choose a scenario

The same R workflow can be used for all three scenarios. Full worksheets are available on Blackboard.

Forestry: comparing plantation types
A forestry organisation is comparing the climatic conditions of its timber-producing forests in northern and southern Europe to understand how forestry practices need to adapt to different environments, particularly in the face of future climate change. They want to know whether the plantation in the north experience a different average yearly precipitation than the plantations in the south. Compare locations of their plantations in Northern Europe with locations in Southern Europe using BIO12 (annual precipitation).

Zoology: comparing two animal species
In Europe, there are two species of grass snake: - the common grass snake (Natrix natrix), which is found in Eastern part of Europe, and the barred grass snake (Natrix helvetica), which is found in the western part of Europe. A zoologist wants to know whether these two animal species are recorded in places with different annual mean temperatures. Compare the groups using BIO1 (annual mean temperature).

Geography and conservation: protected and unprotected places
A conservation team wants to know whether the protected areas in Europe are a good representation of the general climate in Europe. Ideally, protected areas should be representative of the total climate in Europe, so that we capture the full range of conditions that species experience. Compare a selection of locations in European protected areas, with a random selection of unprotected areas to see if there is a difference in climate, specifically in terms of precipitation BIO12 (annual precipitation)

Load and inspect the sample coordinates

The coordinate file contains one row per sample. It has four columns:

Column Meaning
sample A unique sample number
lat Latitude in decimal degrees
lon Longitude in decimal degrees
group The group to which the sample belongs (A or B in the practice file, but will be different in your file)

Read the CSV file

Map the sample locations

A map helps us check where the sample locations are and whether the two groups are geographically separated.

First, we extract the two groups into separate data frames so that we can plot each using different colours. Then we can plot the sample locations on a map of Europe. Remember to change the label A and B to the names of your groups.

# first extract the two groups into separate data frames filtering for either "A" or "B" in the group column.
# REMEMBER TO CHANGE THE LABELS A AND B TO THE NAMES OF YOUR GROUPS
samples_A <- subset(samples, group == "A")
samples_B <- subset(samples, group == "B")


# plot the map first
# The xlim and ylim arguments crop the map to the area of interest (in this case, Europe)
map("world", xlim = c(-15, 55), ylim = c(30, 70))

# add sample locations for group A (as red circles)
points(samples_A$lon, samples_A$lat, pch = 19, col = "tomato")

# add sample locations for group B (as blue triangles)
points(samples_B$lon, samples_B$lat, pch = 17, col = "dodgerblue")

# Remember to change the labels "A" and "B" to the names of your groups
legend("bottomleft", legend = c("A", "B"), pch = 19, col = c("tomato", "dodgerblue"), title = "Group name")

Extract climate for each sample location

We will now use the function location_slice() to extract climate values for the sample coordinates.

We will extract either “bio01” or “bio12”. BIO1 is the annual mean temperature, and BIO12 is the annual precipitation. You will focus on just one variable for your statistical comparison, depending on which scenario you chose. In this example, it’s bio01.

climate_data <- location_slice(
  x = samples,                # data frame containing the coordinates and group information
  coords = c("lon", "lat"),   # names of the columns containing the coordinates
  time_bp = 0,                # time slice to extract (0 = present-day)
  bio_variables = "bio01",    # climate variable to extract (bio01 or bio12)
  dataset = "Example"         # climate dataset (this is always "Example" for this practical)
)

We can now look at the temperature bio01 or precipitation bio12 for each sample location. Each row corresponds to one sample location.

# Look at the climate values
summary(climate_data$bio01)
##    Min. 1st Qu.  Median    Mean 3rd Qu.    Max. 
##   2.476   9.035  10.736  11.010  14.252  15.918
